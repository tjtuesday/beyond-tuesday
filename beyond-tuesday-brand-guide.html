What will not change

The name is set in Libre Franklin, in caps. BEYOND is heavy. TUESDAY is lighter and tracked a little wider. That wordmark stands in for the logo until the real one lands, and it will stay part of the logo afterward.

Clear spaceLeave room on every side equal to the height of the B. Nothing else goes inside that space.
Minimum size140 pixels wide on screen. 1.25 inches in print. Smaller than that, use the name in running text instead.
ColorNavy on white. White on navy or orange. Never set the name in orange.

Color

Navy is for text and structure. Orange is for one accent on a page. Click any swatch to copy its hex code.

How to use them

Navy is for headlines, body text and anything structural. Orange is for one thing on a page: a rule, a highlighted line or a single number. Two orange elements on a page is usually too many.

The CMYK values are conversions from the hex codes. Check them against a proof before a large print run.

Typography

Libre Franklin, used four ways. It is a revival of Franklin Gothic, the newspaper gothic. It holds up in heavy caps and stays readable small.

Voice

Five rules for anything written under the name.

Short declarative sentences.
One idea per sentence. If a sentence needs a comma to breathe, split it.
Plain words. No jargon.
Write for a busy reader. Use the word you would say out loud.
Say what to do, then why.
Lead with the action and put the reasoning after it.
AP style.
No Oxford comma. No em dashes. Numerals for 10 and up. Titles capitalized only before a name.
Sound like a person.
No stacked adjectives, no throat-clearing, no sentence that could open any press release.
Instead ofWe leverage a holistic, cross-functional approach to deliver scalable outcomes for our stakeholders.
WriteHere is what we do, how long it takes and what it costs.

The name is two words in caps in the wordmark. In running text it is Beyond Tuesday. No all caps, no hyphen.
